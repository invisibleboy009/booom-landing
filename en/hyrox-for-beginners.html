Hyrox for beginners

What Hyrox is, how the 8 runs and 8 stations work, which categories there are and how to prepare — even if you have never raced before.

What is Hyrox?

Hyrox is a standardised fitness race that combines running and functional exercises. It was founded in Germany in 2017 and now runs all over the world. What makes it special is that the format is always the same, so your time in London can be compared directly with a time from Berlin or New York.

The race takes place indoors and has 8 rounds. Each round is a 1 km run + 1 functional station. In total you run 8 km and complete 8 stations.

The format: 8 runs and 8 stations

The stations always come in this order:

  1. SkiErg — 1,000 m
  2. Sled Push — 50 m
  3. Sled Pull — 50 m
  4. Burpee Broad Jumps (a burpee followed by a forward jump) — 80 m
  5. Rowing — 1,000 m
  6. Farmers Carry (carrying two weights) — 200 m
  7. Sandbag Lunges — 100 m
  8. Wall Balls (throwing a ball to a target on the wall) — 75 or 100 reps

Between a run and a station is the so-called Roxzone — a transition area where a surprising amount of time gets lost. That is exactly why a planned pace pays off.

Categories

CategoryWho it is for
OpenEveryday athletes, lighter weights — ideal for your first race
ProExperienced athletes, heavier weights at the stations
DoublesIn pairs — you split the stations and run together
RelayA team of 4 — the easiest way in for a group of friends

If it is your first time, go for Open or Doubles. Nothing to be shy about — the goal is to finish and enjoy it.

How long does it take?

Most beginners finish Hyrox in 75–120 minutes. The elite finish in under 60 minutes. What decides it is mostly your running fitness and how well you can run tired after the stations.

How to prepare

1. Run — including when you are tired

The biggest difference in Hyrox is compromised running — running straight after a strength station, when your legs feel like lead. Add sessions like: 400–1,000 m run → station → run again. Your body learns to run under fatigue.

2. A strength base

Squats, deadlifts, presses and above all legs will help you on the sleds, the lunges and the wall balls. You do not need a one-rep max, you need strength endurance.

3. Practise the stations themselves

Wall balls and burpees are the hardest for most people — practise the technique and your breathing. The sleds need explosive legs and the right body angle.

4. Have a pacing plan

Do not start too fast. Split your target time into runs and stations in advance — the Hyrox pacing calculator does it in a few seconds.

Frequently asked questions

What is Hyrox?

Hyrox is a standardised fitness race that combines running with functional exercises. You complete 8 rounds, each one a 1 km run followed by one functional station, so 8 km of running and 8 stations in total. The format is the same everywhere in the world, so you can compare your times with any race.

Do I need to be very fit to do Hyrox?

No. Hyrox is designed for everyday athletes, not only elite ones. There is an Open category with lighter weights and a Doubles format where you split the stations with a partner. A basic level of fitness and a few months of preparation are enough.

How long does a Hyrox race take?

Most beginners finish Hyrox in roughly 75 to 120 minutes. Elite athletes finish in under 60 minutes. Your time depends mostly on your running fitness and how well you handle the stations while tired.

How should I prepare for Hyrox?

Combine running, strength training and so-called compromised running, which means running straight after a strength station so your body gets used to running while fatigued. Practise the individual stations (wall balls, sleds, burpees) and work on your pacing. A pacing plan that splits your target time into runs and stations helps.
