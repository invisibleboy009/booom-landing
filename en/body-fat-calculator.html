Body fat calculator

What counts as a healthy range, how to measure body fat (and which method is accurate), and how to bring it down gradually — without crash diets.

Body fat calculator (US Navy method)

What is body fat percentage and why it matters

Body fat percentage tells you what share of your body weight is fat (the rest is muscle, bone, water and organs). It's a better indicator than weight or BMI alone — two people with the same weight can look completely different depending on how much muscle and fat they carry.

What counts as a healthy range

CategoryMenWomen
Essential fat3–5%10–13%
Athletes6–13%14–20%
Fitness14–17%21–24%

Excess25%+32%+

Women naturally carry a higher body fat percentage than men — it's physiologically necessary. Ranges vary slightly by age and source.

Measurement methods — and how accurate they are

MethodAccuracy / note
DEXA scanMost accurate, but expensive and needs a dedicated device
Skinfold calipersGood with an experienced tester and the same person each time
Circumference method (US Navy)Free, just a tape measure — a decent estimate
Bioimpedance (scales)Rough estimate, fluctuates with hydration
Photo / AI analysisQuick and convenient at home, good for tracking trends

Important: no at-home method is perfectly accurate. So don't chase the absolute number — follow the trend over time. Always measure with the same method and under the same conditions (e.g. in the morning, fasted).

How to lower body fat

  • A moderate calorie deficit — not starvation. Work it out in the calorie calculator.
  • Enough protein (~2 g/kg) — it preserves muscle and keeps you full
  • Strength training — so you lose fat, not muscle
  • Movement and sleep — recovery and NEAT make a bigger difference than you think
  • Patience — a realistic, sustainable pace is ~0.5–1% of body weight per week

How to measure your circumferences properly

The US Navy method is only as accurate as your measuring. Use a soft tailor's tape and follow these rules:

  1. Height: barefoot, standing straight.
  2. Neck: just below the larynx, tape level, don't squeeze it and don't tilt your head forward.
  3. Waist: men at navel level, women at the narrowest point of the torso. Relax and breathe out, don't suck in your stomach.
  4. Hips (women): at the widest point of the buttocks.
  5. Measure three times and use the average. Same time and conditions (ideally in the morning, fasted) for every measurement.

Worked example

Man, height 180 cm, waist 85 cm, neck 38 cm — comes out at roughly 16.1%, which is the Fitness range.

Woman, height 168 cm, waist 74 cm, hips 98 cm, neck 32 cm — comes out at roughly 27.6%, which is the Average range.

If you also enter your weight, the calculator works out your fat mass and lean mass. When tracking progress, how your lean mass (muscle) changes is more useful than the percentage alone.

Where the method falls short

  • Very muscular people often have a thicker neck and waist, so the estimate can differ slightly from reality.
  • A single measurement doesn't show a trend. Watch how it develops over several weeks.
  • Other methods (DEXA, calipers) give a different number. Don't switch between them if you want to compare over time.

Frequently asked questions

What body fat percentage is healthy?

For men, the healthy fitness range is generally considered to be around 10–20%, and for women 18–28%. Essential fat (the minimum the body needs to function) is around 3–5% for men and 10–13% for women. Values vary with age and measurement method.

How can you measure body fat percentage?

The most common methods are skinfold calipers, bioimpedance (scales and devices), the circumference method (US Navy), a DEXA scan and photo/AI analysis. DEXA is the most accurate, the others are estimates — the trend over time with the same method matters more than the absolute number.

How does the US Navy method calculator work?

It estimates your body fat percentage from your height and neck and waist circumference (plus hips for women) using a formula developed by the US Navy. All you need is a tailor's tape, and although the result is only an estimate with an error of a few percentage points, it's practically useful for tracking trends.

Are body fat scales accurate?

Bioimpedance scales only give a rough estimate that fluctuates with hydration, food and time of day. To track a trend, always measure under the same conditions (e.g. in the morning, fasted). Don't treat the absolute number as exact.

What body fat percentage do I need for visible abs?

As a rough guide, abs are clearly visible below about 12 to 15% for men and below 18 to 22% for women, but it's very individual — it depends on where you store fat, muscle size and genetics.

How often should I measure body fat?

Once every 2 to 4 weeks is enough, always with the same method and under the same conditions. Measuring every day just creates noise, because the result fluctuates with hydration and food.

How do I lower my body fat percentage?

A moderate calorie deficit, enough protein (~2 g/kg), strength training to keep muscle, and plenty of movement and sleep. Long-term consistency is the key, not extreme diets — those lead to muscle loss and yo-yo dieting.
