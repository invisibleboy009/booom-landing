8-Week Hyrox Training Plan: A Sample Plan for Beginners

Updated September 20, 2026 · 8 min read

Eight weeks is enough to finish your first Hyrox with a smile, if you can already run and you’re not a total newcomer to the gym. Here is a plan that shows you what to do each week.

Who the plan is for

  • You can run 5 km in one go, even slowly.
  • You train 3 to 4 times a week and know the basic exercises (squat, deadlift, lunges, presses).
  • You have 8 weeks and your goal is to finish your first Hyrox, not to win your category.

If you don’t meet these conditions, add a base phase at the start. If you have an injury or health limitations, talk to a doctor. The plan is a sample, so adapt it to your time and abilities.

Hyrox in brief

Hyrox is 8 rounds, each made up of 1 km of running and one functional station. The stations come in this order: SkiErg, Sled Push, Sled Pull, Burpee Broad Jumps, Rowing, Farmers Carry, Sandbag Lunges and Wall Balls. You can find more about the format in the article Hyrox for beginners (Slovak). Weights depend on the category and season, so check them in the official rules.

If you have 4 or 12 weeks

Eight weeks is a sample, not a rule. You can adapt the plan to less or more time without changing the weekly structure.

In a longer build, add a deload week roughly every third or fourth week. Log your sessions in a training log, and if fatigue keeps piling up, read the article on recovery and sleep.

Practising the stations: technique in brief

  • SkiErg: pull from your hips and trunk, not just your arms. Keep an even rhythm.
  • Sled Push: low centre of gravity, short and quick steps, keep it smooth.
  • Sled Pull: your legs and trunk do the work, your hands only pass the rope along.
  • Burpee Broad Jumps: short jumps in rhythm, save your breath and your arms.
  • Rowing: legs first, then trunk, arms last.
  • Farmers Carry: firm grip, short steps, don’t put the weights down unless you have to.
  • Sandbag Lunges: knees under control, the bag stable on your shoulders.
  • Wall Balls: a smooth squat and throw, short sets with short pauses.

Gear: just the essentials

You don’t need special equipment, just things that won’t get in your way and that you have time to test.

  • Shoes: you will run 8 km and do every station in one pair, so look for a compromise: a stable running shoe with moderate cushioning and a sole that grips on a smooth floor during the sled push.
  • Grip: Sled Pull, Farmers Carry and Wall Balls tax your forearms. Include farmers carries and bar hangs. Rules on chalk and gloves differ by event, so check yours.
  • Clothing: breathable and non-chafing during lunges and burpees. The sled surface can scrape your knees and shins, so longer socks or sleeves can help.

The most common first-timer mistakes

  • Only running or only lifting. Hyrox is about the switch between them. Your legs behave differently after the sled push, so do one hybrid session a week.
  • The first simulation on race day. Include at least a half simulation to learn what pace you can hold. You can see live splits in, for example, the Hyrox simulator in the BOOOM app.
  • Neglected station technique. A badly done Sled Pull or Wall Ball costs you more energy than another kilometre. Go through the technique for each station.
  • No target time. Work it out in the Hyrox pacing calculator.
  • Cramming in the last week. A hard session three days before the race adds fatigue, not fitness.

The last week and race day

  • Don’t try new things. Food, shoes and clothing must be proven in training.
  • Sleep. In the last days before the race, sleep is the best preparation.
  • Start slowly. The first kilometre feels easy on adrenaline, and you’ll pay for an overeager pace in the second half.
  • Hold your pacing. Work out your target pace in the Hyrox pacing calculator.

Nutrition and hydration before the race

These are general principles, not individual advice. If you have a medical condition, an eating disorder or a special diet, sort out your food with a professional.

  • Race week: eat as usual and don’t start a cut. Estimate maintenance intake in the calorie calculator and keep protein in line with the protein article.
  • Last 2 to 3 days: slightly more carbohydrates (rice, potatoes, pasta, bread) at the expense of fat and fibre.
  • Race morning: eat 2 to 3 hours before the start. Carbohydrates, little fat and fibre, something familiar, such as oatmeal with a banana or white bread with jam.
  • Drinking: drink steadily through the day, not a lot at once just before the start. Pale urine is a rough indicator. During the race, take small sips whenever you get the chance.
  • No experiments: leave new gels, drinks and supplements for training. After the race, replace protein, carbohydrates and fluids.

Race day checklist

  1. The evening before: pack your bag, shoes, clothing and water, and check the start time and place. Go to bed on time.
  2. 3 hours before: a light meal following the principles above.
  3. 2 hours before: arrive, pick up your number, drop your things, and look over the transition zone and the order of stations.
  4. 45 minutes before: warm up: 5 to 10 minutes of easy jogging, dynamic mobility for hips, ankles and shoulders, a few squats and ball throws. Nothing tiring.
  5. 10 minutes before: toilet, a few sips of water, calm breathing, and run through your pacing plan in your head.
  6. During the race: start each station with a breath and good technique, not a sprint. Keep the run between stations at a calm pace and sip water.
  7. After the finish: walk for a few minutes, then water and a meal with protein and carbohydrates. Write down your splits and how you felt while it’s fresh.

How to track progress

After each simulation, write down the splits of all runs and stations. You’ll see where you lose time and can focus your training there. Track strength progress in the 1RM calculator and in your training log.

FAQ

Are 8 weeks enough for a first Hyrox?

If you can run 5 km in one go and train at least 3 times a week, yes, for finishing your first race. If not, extend the base phase.

How much should I run per week?

In this plan roughly 15 to 25 km per week, most of it at an easy pace. Consistency matters more than kilometres.

Can I prepare at home?

Partly: runs and strength training with dumbbells and bodyweight. Try the specific stations (SkiErg, sled, rowing) in a gym at least a few times before the race.

How should I prepare for pacing?

Use the Hyrox pacing calculator: enter your target time and you get run paces and station times. After the simulation, replace them with your actual splits.

Can you prepare for Hyrox in 4 weeks?

If you already run at least 3 times a week and do strength training, yes. It is more about sharpening your form than building a base. If you are starting from scratch, choose 12 weeks or move your race date.

What should you eat on the morning of a Hyrox?

Roughly 2 to 3 hours before the start, eat a meal rich in carbohydrates with little fat and fibre, for example oatmeal with a banana or white bread with jam. Pick food you know from training.
